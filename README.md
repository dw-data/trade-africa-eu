# How has Africa-EU trade evolved in the past 25 years?
The 11th OACPS Summit in March 2026 in Malabo marks the 50th anniversary of the foundation of the Organisation of African, Caribbean and Pacific States (OACPS) and the first Lomé Convention, one of the oldest partnerships between the EU and third countries. 

In this story, we explore how trade has evolved between Africa, particularly African ACP countries, and who has benefited from these agreements.

# Data sources
We have extracted the data from Eurostat's database "EU trade since 1988 by HS2-4-6 and CN8", which includes detailed data. We have used the most recently available data, last updated on 13/02/2026. More information on this database metadata can be found <a href='https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/ext_go_detail_sims.htm'>here</a>. Eurostat is the statistical office of the European Union. 

# Statistical dimensions
Scope: We extracted all trade flows (exports and imports) by goods, not including services. We have only considered the main classification chapters of product codes in our analysis, and remained at 2-digit level of the Combined Nomenclature (HS2).

Reference period: We initially considered all years with available data (1999 on), but eventually considered since 2000 on in our charts. We looked into annual data. 

Reporting country: The European Union as defined after Brexit 27 countries). 

Partner country: All African countries, including past definitions

Trade value (in euros): As defined by Eurostat, is "statistical value, i.e. the amount that would be invoiced in the event of sale or purchase at the national border of the reporting country." Exports from the EU to Africa are a FOB (Free on Board) valuation and imports in the EU from Africa are a CIF (Cost Insurance Freight) valuation.
